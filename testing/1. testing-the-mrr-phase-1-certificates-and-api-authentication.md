## 1. Introduction

OfDIA are taking an incremental approach to testing the technical capabilities of the machine-readable register (MRR). Rather than waiting for the complete service to become available, we are inviting digital verification services (DVS) providers to begin testing individual capabilities as they are delivered. This will help validate the technical implementation, identify issues early and incorporate feedback from participating organisations.

**This first exercise focuses on certificates and API authentication.**

You will access a sandbox environment, view basic information about your organisation and its registered services, obtain assets for one of those services and use them to authenticate and make an API request. You can choose between two authentication methods: mutual TLS (mTLS) or a signed client assertion (`private_key_jwt`).

### Why are we using the Raidiam sandbox?

The MRR user interface and onboarding journeys are currently being developed. To enable technical testing to begin sooner, we are providing access to the underlying Raidiam platform, which supports the MRR's technical capabilities.

**This is not the final MRR user interface or onboarding experience.** You will have opportunities to test the MRR interface and user journeys separately as they become available.

### How long will this take?

We expect this initial exercise to take approximately **1 to 2 hours** for a technical team member familiar with certificates and API requests. You will need access to a terminal, OpenSSL and an HTTP API client such as Postman or curl. The instructions cover macOS, Linux and Windows.

### What comes next?

We are introducing testing capabilities incrementally. From mid-November, we expect to begin looking at **OpenID Federation** artefacts, including Entity Configurations and trust-chain resolution. Separate instructions will be provided for these activities.

This first exercise validates authentication against the sandbox APIs only. Later exercises will introduce federation and trust validation, which will underpin interactions between DVS providers, and between DVS providers and public authorities.

### Questions and support

If you have any questions during testing, contact: [digital.identity.register@dsit.gov.uk](mailto:digital.identity.register.gov.uk).

## 2. Getting access to the sandbox

### 1. Nominate your testers

Send the following to [digital.identity.register@dsit.gov.uk](mailto:digital.identity.register.gov.uk):

- Your organisation's name.
- The names and email addresses of one or two technical team members who will participate in testing.

We will create your organisation in the sandbox and configure the nominated individuals as **Organisation Administrators**. Your organisation and all of its services listed on the digital verification services register will be preconfigured using publicly available register information.

You do not need to nominate a service in advance. You do not need to complete an organisation onboarding process yourself.

### Step 2: Receive your invitation

Once access has been configured, you will receive an email inviting you to access the **DVS Register Sandbox**. The email will confirm your access and contain a **Go to the platform** button. Select this button to open the Raidiam sandbox.

### Step 3: Register your account

When you reach the Raidiam sign-in page, select **Register**. Complete the registration form with the requested information, including:

- First name and family name.
- Email address. Use the email address nominated for sandbox access.
- Phone number.
- Password and password confirmation.
- Date of birth and any other required registration fields.

Select **Register** to continue.

### Step 4: Verify your account and sign in

Complete the one-time passcode (OTP) verification process when prompted. Once registration and verification are complete, return to the sign-in page if necessary and sign in using your email address and password.

You should now have access to the Raidiam sandbox as an Organisation Administrator for your allocated organisation.

## 3. Explore your organisation

Once signed in, navigate to your organisation in the sandbox. You should be able to view basic information about your organisation and its registered services, pre-populated using publicly available register data.

> [!NOTE]
> This is a sandbox environment intended for technical testing. Some information may be incomplete or contain placeholder values and should be treated as illustrative rather than an accurate representation of production data. You do not need to review or correct any discrepancies as part of this exercise.

In the sandbox, each registered service is represented as an application. Where these instructions refer to an application, this means the sandbox record for one of your registered services.

Select any one of your available applications to complete the exercise. You do not need to test every service. The purpose of this step is simply to confirm that you can access your organisation and proceed with technical testing.

## 4. Prepare your assets

### Why this matters

The machine-readable register will let organisations check a DVS provider's registered status programmatically, rather than by searching the GOV.UK list. To use the register's APIs, and in future to interact with other registered DVS providers and public authorities, a service needs a way to prove its identity to other systems.

Your service's assets do this. They are issued to, or registered against, a specific registered service, not your organisation as a whole. When your service connects to a register API, they show which service is connecting and that it is known to the register.

In this section you will prepare assets for one of your registered services, using a private key that you keep yourself.

### Choose your authentication method

You can authenticate using either mutual TLS (mTLS) or a signed client assertion (`private_key_jwt`). Use whichever is your preferred method. Follow the matching option below to prepare your assets, then use the same option in Section 5. You only need to complete one option.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Private key security**
>
> Generating keys on your own machine is acceptable for this sandbox exercise. For production, generate and store private keys in line with National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) guidance, for example using a hardware security module (HSM) or cloud key management service with non-exportable keys, restricted access and planned key rotation.
>
> This applies to both your certificate key (Option A) and your signing key (Option B). Sandbox keys and certificates must never be reused in production.

### Option A: Generate a client transport certificate (mTLS)

Follow these steps if you have chosen mTLS.

#### Step 1: Select the application for your service

From your organisation in the Raidiam sandbox, select **Applications** and choose the application that represents the registered service you want to test.

#### Step 2: Create a new certificate

Select **App Certificates** > **New Certificate**.

#### Step 3: Select the certificate type

Select the **DVS Client Transport** certificate type and select **Continue**. Make sure you select the DVS-specific client transport profile rather than a generic Raidiam certificate type.

#### Step 4: Provide a description

Enter a description explaining the certificate's purpose, for example:

> OfDIA MRR Sandbox - Client Transport Certificate Testing

Select **Continue**.

#### Step 5: Generate your Certificate Signing Request (CSR)

The Raidiam interface will display an OpenSSL command for generating your private key and Certificate Signing Request (CSR).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Do not use the OpenSSL command displayed in the sandbox interface. The commands have recently been updated, and the versions in this guide are the current ones.

The command below is required to meet the OfDIA certificate profile requirements, including Subject Alternative Name (SAN) information. Replace each placeholder in angle brackets with your own values, which are shown in the sandbox for your organisation and application:

- `<COUNTRY>`: your organisation's country of registration, as a two-letter code. For DVS providers this will likely be `UK`.
- `<LEGAL_NAME>`: your organisation's legal entity name. You will find it in the **Business Information** section of your Organisation page.
- `<ORG_ID>`: your organisation ID, an integer. It is labelled **Organisation ID** near the top of your Organisation page, with a copy button beside it.
- `<SS_ID>`: your application ID, a UUID. It is shown on your application's tile, and as **Application ID** at the top of the application's Details page. Both have a copy button.
- `<FEDERATION_ENTITY_ID>`: your application's federation entity identifier. Construct it as `https://rp.sandbox.directory.access-dvs-register.service.gov.uk/openid_relying_party/` followed by your application ID (`<SS_ID>`).

```bash
openssl req -new -newkey rsa:2048 -utf8 -sha256 \
  -keyout <SS_ID>-dvs_client_transport.key \
  -out <SS_ID>-dvs_client_transport.csr \
  -subj "/C=<COUNTRY>/O=<LEGAL_NAME>/OU=<ORG_ID>/CN=<SS_ID>" \
  -addext "keyUsage=critical,digitalSignature" \
  -addext "basicConstraints=critical,CA:FALSE" \
  -addext "extendedKeyUsage=clientAuth" \
  -addext "subjectAltName = URI:<FEDERATION_ENTITY_ID>"
```

**Windows users:** You can run the command using Git Bash, which includes OpenSSL, Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL), or a standalone OpenSSL installation. If you use Git Bash, prefix the command with `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1` so that the certificate subject (`-subj`) is not altered.

The commands in this guide are split over several lines, with a backslash at the end of each line. This works in Git Bash and WSL. In PowerShell, replace each backslash with a backtick. In Command Prompt, replace it with a caret (`^`).

Run the command in your terminal. OpenSSL will ask you to set a pass phrase for your private key. Choose one and keep it safe. You will need it whenever you use the key.

The command will generate:

- A **private key**, which must be retained securely and will be required when testing your certificate.
- A **CSR file**, which you will upload to the Raidiam sandbox.

Do not share your private key with Raidiam, OfDIA or other participants.

#### Step 6: Upload your CSR and obtain your certificate

Return to the Raidiam sandbox.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Upload the `.csr` file, not the `.key` file. Your private key must never leave your machine.

1. Upload the generated CSR file in PEM format.
2. Select **Continue**.
3. Once the request has been validated and processed, select **Done**.
4. Download your issued client transport certificate.

The platform validates your CSR and submits it to the Certificate Authority (CA) for issuance.

**Expected outcome:** You have successfully generated a DVS client transport certificate and retained the corresponding private key. You are now ready to authenticate to the sandbox authorisation server and make an authenticated API request.

#### Further information

For additional background, see [Raidiam developer documentation: Manage Application Certificates](https://www.raidiam.com/developers/docs/how-tos/certificates/manage-application-certificates).

### Option B: Prepare your signing assets (`private_key_jwt`)

> [!NOTE]
> You do not need a client transport certificate for this option.

With `private_key_jwt`, your service authenticates using a digitally signed JSON Web Token (JWT). You create the JWT using a private signing key, and the authorisation server verifies it using the corresponding public key.

#### Step 1: Select the application for your service

From your organisation in the Raidiam sandbox, select **Applications** and choose the application that represents the registered service you want to test.

#### Step 2: Generate or register your signing key

Your application needs a signing key pair:

- A **private key**, which you keep securely and use to sign JWTs.
- A corresponding **public key**, which is made available to the authorisation server.

Generate your private signing key and a Certificate Signing Request (CSR) using the command below. Replace the placeholders as described in Option A, Step 5:

```bash
openssl req -new -newkey rsa:2048 -utf8 -nodes -sha256 \
  -keyout <SS_ID>-dvs_client_signing.key \
  -out <SS_ID>-dvs_client_signing.csr \
  -subj "/C=<COUNTRY>/O=<LEGAL_NAME>/OU=<ORG_ID>/CN=<SS_ID>" \
  -addext "keyUsage=digitalSignature" \
  -addext "subjectAltName = URI:<FEDERATION_ENTITY_ID>"
```

The Windows guidance in Option A, Step 5 also applies here. Then upload the CSR in the sandbox as described in Option A, Steps 2 to 6, selecting the **DVS Client Signing** certificate type.

#### Step 3: Confirm your public key is available

Raidiam publishes application signing keys through a JSON Web Key Set (JWKS) endpoint. Find your application's `jwks_uri` and confirm that your public signing key is listed.

#### Step 4: Confirm your authentication configuration

Your application's software statement must have its token endpoint authentication method set to `private_key_jwt`. Check this in your application's details before requesting an access token. If it is set to a different value, contact Tim Saunders.

**Expected outcome:** Your application has a signing key for creating signed client assertions, and the corresponding public key is registered for verification.

## 5. Authenticate and make an API request

### Why this matters

In this section you will use the assets you prepared in Section 4 to authenticate to the sandbox authorisation server, obtain an access token, and call a protected API to retrieve your own organisation's details.

This is the basic pattern a DVS provider will use to query the machine-readable register as part of its own service. The same building blocks are expected to support future interactions between registered DVS providers, and between DVS providers and public authorities, once federation and trust validation are introduced in later exercises.

This exercise tests authentication only. Follow the option you chose in Section 4.

> [!NOTE]
> The Option A endpoints below are the sandbox endpoints. The Option B token endpoint shown is an example. The confirmed endpoint will be provided before testing begins.

### Option A: Mutual TLS (mTLS)

With mTLS, your service presents its client transport certificate when it opens a secure connection. The authorisation server checks that the certificate was issued by the register's certificate authority to your registered service before it issues an access token.

Use the same certificate and private key for both the token request and the API call. This option uses the certificate and private key you prepared in Section 4, Option A.

#### Step 1: Check you have everything you need

Before starting, make sure you have:

- Your issued **DVS Client Transport Certificate** (`.pem`).
- The corresponding **private key** (`.key`) generated in Section 4, and its pass phrase.
- Your **Client ID**. See Step 2.
- The sandbox **CA bundle** (`cacert.pem`), created as described below.
- An HTTP client such as curl or Postman.

##### Create the sandbox CA bundle

Your HTTP client needs the sandbox's CA certificates to trust the sandbox servers. Download the two certificates from these public URLs:

- <http://crl.pki.sandbox.directory.access-dvs-register.service.gov.uk/issuer-ca.pem>
- <http://crl.pki.sandbox.directory.access-dvs-register.service.gov.uk/root-ca.pem>

Then combine them into a single file:

```bash
cat issuer-ca.pem root-ca.pem > cacert.pem
```

In Command Prompt, use:

```bat
type issuer-ca.pem root-ca.pem > cacert.pem
```

**Windows users:** In Windows PowerShell, `curl` runs a different command, so type `curl.exe`. The version of curl built into Windows may not accept a separate private key file. If so, convert your certificate and key into a PKCS#12 (`.p12`) file or use Postman. Conversion instructions are available on request.

#### Step 2: Obtain an access token

You will use your certificate and private key to authenticate with the sandbox authorisation server using OAuth 2.0 Client Credentials and mTLS.

Run the following command in your terminal, replacing the placeholders as follows:

- `<MTLS-TOKEN-ENDPOINT>`: `https://matls-auth.sandbox.directory.access-dvs-register.service.gov.uk/token`
- `<YOUR_CLIENT_ID>`: your federation entity identifier, constructed as in Section 4, Option A, Step 5: `https://rp.sandbox.directory.access-dvs-register.service.gov.uk/openid_relying_party/` followed by your application ID.
- `/path/to/cacert.pem`: the CA bundle you created in Step 1.

If your private key is protected with a pass phrase, enter it when prompted, or supply it with `--pass`.

```bash
curl --request POST \
  '<MTLS-TOKEN-ENDPOINT>' \
  --cert /path/to/certificate.pem \
  --key /path/to/private.key \
  --cacert /path/to/cacert.pem \
  --header 'Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded' \
  --data-urlencode 'grant_type=client_credentials' \
  --data-urlencode 'client_id=<YOUR_CLIENT_ID>' \
  --data-urlencode 'scope=directory:software'
```

**Expected result:** The authorisation server returns an HTTP 200 response containing an OAuth access token. For example:

```json
{
  "access_token": "<ACCESS_TOKEN>",
  "token_type": "Bearer",
  "expires_in": 3600
}
```

The token expiry shown is illustrative. The actual value may differ.

#### Step 3: Make an authenticated API request

Use your access token to retrieve information about your organisation through the Raidiam API.

Run the following command, replacing the placeholders as follows:

- `<API-BASE-URL>/<GET-ORGANISATION-PATH>`: `matls-dirapi.sandbox.directory.access-dvs-register.service.gov.uk/organisations/` followed by your organisation ID (`<ORG_ID>` from Section 4).
- `<ACCESS_TOKEN>`: the access token returned in Step 2.

```bash
curl --request GET \
  'https://<API-BASE-URL>/<GET-ORGANISATION-PATH>' \
  --cert /path/to/certificate.pem \
  --key /path/to/private.key \
  --cacert /path/to/cacert.pem \
  --header 'Authorization: Bearer <ACCESS_TOKEN>'
```

Use the **same certificate and private key** that you used to obtain your access token.

**Expected result:** The API returns HTTP 200 and a JSON response containing details of your organisation. This demonstrates that your application can authenticate using its transport certificate, obtain an access token and use that token to access a protected API.

### Option B: Signed client assertion (`private_key_jwt`)

This option uses the signing key you prepared in Section 4, Option B. The authorisation server checks the signature on your signed client assertion against the public key registered for your service before issuing an access token.

#### Step 1: Create a signed client assertion

Create a short-lived JWT containing the required claims: client identification, audience, issue time, expiry and a unique identifier. Sign the JWT using your application's private signing key.

#### Step 2: Request an access token

Send the signed JWT to the sandbox token endpoint using the Client Credentials grant, with the `client_assertion_type` and `client_assertion` parameters. Use the standard token endpoint, for example `https://auth.sandbox.raidiam.io/token`, not the mTLS endpoint used in Option A. You do not need to present a client certificate.

The authorisation server verifies the signature against your application's registered public key. For example, replacing the placeholders with your own details:

```bash
curl --request POST \
  '<TOKEN-ENDPOINT>' \
  --header 'Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded' \
  --data-urlencode 'grant_type=client_credentials' \
  --data-urlencode 'client_id=<YOUR_CLIENT_ID>' \
  --data-urlencode 'scope=directory:software' \
  --data-urlencode \
    'client_assertion_type=urn:ietf:params:oauth:client-assertion-type:jwt-bearer' \
  --data-urlencode 'client_assertion=<SIGNED_JWT>'
```

**Expected result:** The authorisation server returns an HTTP 200 response containing an OAuth access token, as in Option A.

#### Step 3: Make an authenticated API request

Use the access token to call the protected API and retrieve your organisation's details. You do not need to present a client certificate.

```bash
curl --request GET \
  'https://<API-BASE-URL>/<GET-ORGANISATION-PATH>' \
  --header 'Authorization: Bearer <ACCESS_TOKEN>'
```

**Expected outcome:** You have authenticated using `private_key_jwt`, obtained an OAuth access token and retrieved your organisation's details.

> [!CAUTION]
> Do not paste private keys into online JWT tools.

### Confirm your results for either option

You have successfully completed the exercise when:

- You have obtained an OAuth access token using your chosen authentication method.
- You have successfully called the protected Raidiam API.
- The API has returned details of your organisation.

If any step fails, record the step, HTTP status code and error message and send them to: [digital.identity.register@dsit.gov.uk](mailto:digital.identity.register.gov.uk) so that the project team can help investigate.

Do not share your private key or access token.

## Reference: Other DVS certificate types

These certificate types are not required for this exercise. The commands are provided for reference. Replace the placeholders as described in Option A, Step 5, and replace `<SERVER_DOMAIN>` with your server's domain name.

### DVS server transport

```bash
openssl req -new -newkey rsa:2048 -utf8 -sha256 \
  -keyout <SS_ID>-dvs_server_signing.key \
  -out <SS_ID>-dvs_server_signing.csr \
  -subj "/C=<COUNTRY>/O=<LEGAL_NAME>/OU=<ORG_ID>/CN=<SS_ID>" \
  -addext "keyUsage=critical,digitalSignature" \
  -addext "basicConstraints=critical,CA:FALSE" \
  -addext "extendedKeyUsage=serverAuth" \
  -addext "subjectAltName = DNS:<SERVER_DOMAIN>"
```

### DVS credential issuer signing

```bash
openssl req -new -newkey rsa:2048 -utf8 -nodes -sha256 \
  -keyout <SS_ID>-dvs_credential_issuer_signing.key \
  -out <SS_ID>-dvs_credential_issuer_signing.csr \
  -subj "/C=<COUNTRY>/O=<LEGAL_NAME>/OU=<ORG_ID>/CN=<SS_ID>" \
  -addext "keyUsage=digitalSignature" \
  -addext "subjectAltName = URI:<FEDERATION_ENTITY_ID>"
```
